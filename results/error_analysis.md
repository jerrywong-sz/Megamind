# Error analysis — Experiment A vs. Experiment B

Generated by `scripts/error_analysis.py` from `robustness_predictions.csv` (471,168 rows = 2 models x 16 conditions x 14,724 validation images).

`experiment_a` is the clean baseline; `experiment_b` is the robustness-augmented model. Label convention: `0` = real, `1` = AI-generated. `prob_ai` is the model's probability that an image is AI-generated; the decision threshold is 0.5.

> **Scope:** CIFAKE only (32x32 images, Stable Diffusion 1.4), validation split. Nothing here speaks to high-resolution images or unseen generators.

## Summary

- **Transformation flips (the core claim): A = 38,504, B = 5,611** across the 15 transformed conditions. Robustness augmentation removes 32,893 flips, 85.4% of the baseline's.
- **Total misclassification events: A = 41,024, B = 9,065** (77.9% fewer).
- **High-confidence mistakes: A = 22,027, B = 972** — a 23x reduction. The baseline is not just wrong more often, it is wrong *emphatically* more often.
- The baseline's failures concentrate in the destructive conditions (noise 0.10, blur 2.0, resize 0.25x), where it approaches or exceeds 50% error; the augmented model degrades gradually instead.
- **On clean images the baseline is slightly better** (143 vs. 195 false positives). The robustness gain is not free — it costs a little clean-set precision.
- Predictions are strongly bimodal for both models, but only the augmented model is well calibrated; see section 7.
- A handful of images fail under *every* condition for the augmented model — likely mislabelled or intrinsically ambiguous rather than robustness failures. See section 5; these are the best candidates for manual inspection.

## 5. Do the same images fail repeatedly?

For each image, how many of the 16 conditions it was misclassified in (images never wrong in any condition are excluded):

| Model | Images wrong at least once | % of all images | Wrong in 1 condition only | Wrong in >=8 conditions | Wrong in all 16 | Median conditions failed |

| experiment_a | 12,993 | 88.2% | 1,774 | 340 | 3 | 3 |
| experiment_b | 2,509 | 17.0% | 904 | 345 | 40 | 2 |

Share of all misclassification events attributable to repeat offenders:

| Model | Total error events | From worst 10% of failing images | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| experiment_a | 41,024 | 8,934 | 21.8% |
| experiment_b | 9,065 | 3,228 | 35.6% |

**Reading:** the two models fail in different shapes. The baseline's errors are broad and condition-driven — most of its images fail somewhere, but usually only in the few destructive conditions, and its failures are scattered across the dataset. The augmented model fails on far fewer images overall, but its failures are more *concentrated*: a larger share of its error events comes from its worst offenders, and 40 of its images are wrong in **all 16** conditions versus only 3 for the baseline. Those 40 look like genuinely mislabelled or intrinsically ambiguous images rather than robustness failures — no amount of augmentation moves them, and they are the natural candidates for manual inspection.

**Reading:** the observation that the model emits extreme values rather than intermediate probabilities is confirmed — only 8.3% (A) and 7.7% (B) of all predictions fall in the middle 0.20-0.80 band, while 12.1% (A) and 7.0% (B) land on exactly 1.0. This is expected from a single-logit network trained with `BCEWithLogitsLoss` to near-zero training loss: the logits grow large and the sigmoid saturates in float32.

The important difference is that bimodality alone is not miscalibration. The augmented model's confidence is *earned* — in its >=0.99 band the actual AI fraction is 0.990, and in its lowest band 0.007, both landing essentially where they should. The baseline's is not: images it scores above 0.9 are AI only 86% of the time, and images it scores below 0.1 are still AI 12% of the time. So the baseline is not merely wrong more often under degradation, its confidence carries much less information about whether it is right. Note this is aggregated over all 16 conditions, so the baseline's poor calibration is driven substantially by the conditions where it collapses.

Practical consequence: `pred` values from the augmented model are usable as probabilities (for thresholding or ranking); the baseline's are not.
